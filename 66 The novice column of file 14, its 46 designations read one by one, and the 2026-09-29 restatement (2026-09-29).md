## 1. What the column is for

File 08 (2026-09-09) sets the rule the column encodes, in Fannie Mae's C1 to C6 condition terms:

| Grade | Rating | Action |
|---|---|---|
| GREEN | C1, C2, C3 built 1980 or later, or C3 with a documented recent renovation | Proceed; inspection confirms |
| YELLOW | C4, or C3 built before 1980 without renovation evidence, or Redfin flood factor 5 or higher | Proceed only with inspection plus written bids; total bids at or under 10% of price; walk if any RED item appears |
| RED | C5, C6, or any RED item (foundation movement, active water or rot, knob-and-tube or aluminum wiring, FPE or Zinsco panels, galvanized or polybutylene supply, failed sewer, termite damage, unpermitted units, open code violations, lead or asbestos abatement, vacant and boarded, uninsurable as-is) | Walk away |

The pipeline README adds one token, GREEN-verify, for a building that meets GREEN only if a claim in the listing (a renovation, new systems) is documented, and the underwriting uses the grade twice: a YELLOW costs four score points and a RED makes the verdict OUT (RED) with vacancy at 8 percent. The `flood` column of file 14 carries the flood factor separately for every row; the `note` column carries the grader's narrative (for the swept rows, the judgment's `cond` line, capped at 300 characters, behind the jurisdiction flag); the `why` column carries the score arithmetic. So the `novice` cell has one job: to state the grade, its evidential basis and its open question in a form a reader can scan down the board and filter on.

## 4. Do the designations communicate optimally? The defects

1. **One grade, many spellings.** GREEN-verify appeared as `GREEN-verify`, `GREEN, verify renovation scope`, `GREEN, verify renovation / photo C3` and `GREEN-verify (...)`; a YELLOW with a verification demand as `YELLOW verify`, `YELLOW-verify`, `YELLOW unverified`, `YELLOW with named targets` and, in the decision boards, `YELLOW (verification demand)`. A filter or a sort on the column cannot group what is one grade.
2. **Four separators, each carrying meaning in one generation only.** ` / photo ` (the folds), `: ` (the by-catch), `, ` (file 08a) and ` (...)` (the hand reads), with `; flood factor` in three positions.
3. **The flood clause was noise where present and missing where it mattered.** Fifty-seven rows carried a factor of 1 to 4, where file 08 is silent; Gilmer Cir's factor 7, a driver and a score point, was not in its cell; and on the swept rows the clause tracked the portal (Redfin pages carry a factor, Zillow pages do not), not the risk. The `flood` column already holds the number for every row.
4. **Four cells of 136 carried a date** (Squirrel x3, Barney). A 9/9 front-photo grade and a 9/25 thirty-frame read looked alike, which is how six stale grades survived on an active board for up to fourteen days.
5. **The evidence basis was implicit.** `photo` meant one listing photo, `frames` an MLS gallery, nothing at all meant the 9/9 front photo; the reader could not tell a one-photo grade from a thirty-frame read without opening the note, and could not see whether an interior or a second unit had ever been shown.
6. **"walk" meant two opposite things** (F6 and F8).
7. **Seven cells lagged the verified layer**, six of them in grade: Poplar (RED since 9/15), Penmar (GREEN-verify since 9/15), Lafayette (YELLOW since 9/15), Eden (YELLOW since 9/16), Hugh Caldwell (YELLOW since 9/16), Ranch Farm (RED pending since 9/19), and Carver Cir ("no photo" against 23 frames read on 9/15). Eleven bare `YELLOW` cells and six `YELLOW / photo C4` cells had reads of 9/15 to 9/23 that existed only in decision boards 32a to 37a.
8. **"Nobody could see anything" had three spellings** (`unrated`, `photo n/a`, `YELLOW unverified`) plus two more in the notes (`UNVERIFIED:`, "graded YELLOW only so the row reaches review").
9. **For the swept rows the cell duplicated the note's grade and said nothing the note did not**, while for the hand-read rows the cell said things the note contradicted (Auburndale, Lafayette).

So the answer to the question asked is: keep the meanings (they were, with the seven exceptions above, the right meanings), and change the form.

## 5. The grammar adopted

```
GRADE: <C-rating and what it was read from>; <why this grade, one clause>; <what is unseen or must be confirmed>[; flood factor N, itself a YELLOW driver] (<date of the read>)
```

- **GRADE** is one of six tokens and nothing else: `GREEN`, `GREEN-verify`, `YELLOW`, `RED`, `RED pending` (a RED item recorded but not yet confirmed by a permit search or an inspection), `not graded` (no photo or record read yet). A row that could not be read is `YELLOW` with "no usable photo" or "no current photo" as its first clause, so the parking grade is visible as such.
- **The C-rating phrase** says what was rated: `C4 on the one listing photo` (a swept row; the photo is the listing's lead image, usually the front), `C4 on the front photo` (the 9/9 grades), `C4 in 14 MLS frames`, `C3 interiors of the 2026 unit, C3 to C4 exterior`, `no usable photo`.
- **The reason** is the grader's own first clause. For a swept row it is the first clause of the judgment's `cond` line (up to the first semicolon outside parentheses), which is why step 6 of the task prompts now tells the grader that the first clause becomes the board cell.
- **The unseen clause** names what the evidence did not cover (`Unit B unseen`, `no interiors`, `lower unit unseen since the 2013 gutted-kitchen frames`) or what must be confirmed (`renovation scope to confirm`, `verify the update scope, oil-tank age and insurance`).
- **The flood clause** appears only at a factor of 5 or more, the file 08 threshold, and says why it is there. RED rows carry the factor without the qualifier.
- **The date** is the date of the read the cell rests on, not the date the cell was written, so a 9/9 grade on the 9/29 board still says 9/9.
