# Regional sweep design: the NC/VA revamp and the Pennsylvania–Ohio–West Virginia–New York expansion

Written 2026-09-28 (cloud session) as the implementation of memo 63's recommendations (§7.1 pipeline fixes, §7.2 widen north in order) for the weekly duplex re-sweep. Companion files, all in this campaign folder: `_pipeline/weekly_task_prompt_{ncva,phila,pitt,ohio}.md` (the four scheduled-task prompts), `_pipeline/sweep_zips_{phila,pitt,ohio}.json` (the ZIP queues), `_pipeline/model/tract_scores_<region>_x1.0.json`, `_x1.25.json`, `tract_watchlist_<region>.csv`, `map_data_<region>.json` (the Gate-1 model for the new counties), `_pipeline/constants_additions_0928.json` (what was merged into `constants.json`), and the code (`weekly_sweep.py`, `sweep_remote.py`, `parsers.py`). The same files are mirrored in the GitHub repository `najumobi/Investment-Outside-NC-VA` under `pipeline_changes/2026-09-28-regions/`, with the build scripts (`acs_build/`) that produced the model. Najum's decisions of 2026-09-28 that shape this: one routine per region on its own weekday; Ohio swept now rather than held; the New Jersey and Delaware counties of the Philadelphia CSA included, New Jersey rows flagged.

## 0. The one-paragraph answer

The Monday routine keeps sweeping the 100 NC/VA ZIPs; three new routines sweep 100 ZIPs each on Tuesday (Philadelphia CSA with its NJ and DE counties, Scranton–Wilkes-Barre, Bloomsburg, Pottsville, Binghamton, Elmira), Wednesday (Pittsburgh CSA, Wheeling, Fairmont–Clarksburg, Morgantown, Parkersburg, Charleston–Huntington, Cumberland MD, Johnstown, Youngstown) and Thursday (Cleveland–Akron–Canton, Toledo, Dayton). Every region is screened the way NC/VA is: by census tract, through the same Gate-1 model, rebuilt from its own formulas and validated to reproduce the existing 4,870-tract NC/VA model exactly (gates 2,341 of 2,341, tiers 4,874 of 4,874), then applied to 5,234 tracts in 93 kept counties with their percentile ranks taken against the NC/VA reference distribution so the two universes score on one scale. Distance is no longer a flat −2 score points beyond 1.25 hours; it is the memo's cost re-specification: a Gate-5 bar of 0.90% (Tier A, ≤ 5.5 Google-equivalent hours), 0.95% (Tier B, ≤ 8.0 h) or 1.05% (Tier C, fly-in), travel dollars and a 4-point management premium in the cash flow for Tiers B and C, and −0.5 in the score beyond 1.25 h so the board's ordering survives. Taxes carry the rental-classification adjustment the projection could not see (Philadelphia +$1,400, other PA +$375, NY +$550 STAR, WV ×2 inside municipalities, OH +0.17 points of price for HB 186). A live smoke test on ten Tuesday ZIPs fetched 20 of 20 pages, parsed 287 listings, geocoded 284, found 218 in passing tracts and shortlisted 84 — and in doing so caught the one defect that would have made the expansion silently return nothing: the Redfin parsers were hard-coded to `NC|VA` addresses.

## 3. The Gate-1 model: rebuilt, validated, extended

**Why it had to be rebuilt.** The tract model in `_pipeline/model/` (4,874 entries: 4,870 NC/VA tracts and four Pasquotank ZCTAs) was produced on 2026-09-08 by `score_tracts.py` in a Temp scratchpad that is not in the campaign folder or Dropbox; only its outputs are. File 04b records the formulas. To score other states on the same footing the scorer was rebuilt from those formulas and checked against every one of the 4,870 tracts before use.

**Inputs pulled** (api.census.gov, ACS 2020-24 5-year, `for=tract:*&in=state:XX`, 2026-09-28, the key served every state on the first attempt): B01003 population; B25003 tenure; B25004_002 vacant for rent; B25024_004/005 2-unit and 3–4-unit structures; B25032_016 renter-occupied units in 2-unit structures; B19013 income; B25064 rent and B25077 value with margins; B25070_007–011 rent burden; B17001 poverty; B23025 labor force; B22010 SNAP; B08303 travel time; B25034 year built; B15003_021–025 associate degree and above; B25038_015 renters who moved in 1989 or earlier; B14007_017/018 college enrolment. Two variable-numbering traps in the 2024 vintage: renter "1989 or earlier" is `B25038_015E` (there is no `_016E`; the renter total is `B25003_003E`), and the model's `college_enrolled_share` divides by total population (B01003), not by the B14007 universe.

**Validation against the 2026-09-08 model** (`acs_build/validate_scorer.py`): every raw metric that enters the gate reproduces with zero difference across all 4,870 tracts (rent, value, tenure, vacancy, rent burden, poverty, employment, commute, year built, SNAP, education, stability, coefficients of variation); the cash-flow chain reproduces exactly (est. duplex value = 1.0× or 1.25× median value; gross rent = 24 × median rent; NOI = 60% of gross; 25% down; 7.0% 30-year debt service; cap rate, GRM, cash flow, cash-on-cash); tenant payment risk = 0.4 × rent-burden + 0.3 × severe-burden + 0.3 × poverty (all in percent), employment stability = 0.6 × employment rate + 0.4 × participation, commute = 40 + 0.6 × short − 0.4 × long, housing quality = 50 + 0.5 × new − 0.5 × old, tenant stability = min(100, 2 × long-tenure renter share) all reproduce exactly. The `norm_` columns are percentile ranks (mean of the strict and weak ranks over n) taken over every tract that carries the metric, not only the fully scorable ones — the residual is 0.0103 points, one half-rank in 4,870. The risk-penalty and quality-bonus coefficients printed rounded in file 04b were refit by least squares on the model's own unclipped rows (n = 4,495 and 4,291): risk penalty = 16.68502 + 0.13905 × TPR − 0.13831 × ES + 0.10432 × EF, clipped to [5.15, 22.5]; quality bonus = 1.57336 + 0.00339 × HQ + 0.06386 × EDU + 0.02022 × TS + 0.04021 × COMMUTE, clipped to [2.65, 9.56]. Enhanced score = clip(base − penalty + bonus + 15, 0, 100) then differs from the model by at most 0.033 points. Gate verdicts agree 2,341 of 2,341. The tier rule, undocumented in 04b, was recovered from the data and matches all 4,874 entries: with the three non-cash-flow margins scaled by their thresholds — (enhanced − 50)/50, (45 − TPR)/45, (ES − 55)/55 — tier 1 needs every scaled margin ≥ 0.25, tier 2 ≥ 0.10, tier 3 ≥ 0 (a PASS), tier 4 ≥ −0.10, tier 5 ≥ −0.25, tier 6 otherwise or any cash-flow failure.

**Extension to the new counties.** 17,781 tracts were pulled for PA, OH, NJ, DE, MD, WV, NY and KY; 5,234 fall in the 93 kept counties of the playbook areas. They are scored with the same code; the one design choice is the percentile universe. Ranking the new tracts among themselves would give a Rust Belt tract a "median" cap rate that is a top-decile cap rate in NC/VA, and ranking everything together would move every NC/VA score and gate. So each new tract's `norm_` values are its percentile against the NC/VA reference distribution at the same multiplier, which leaves NC/VA untouched and makes an enhanced score of 70 mean the same thing in Scranton as in Petersburg. Result at 1.0×: 3,271 PASS, 1,557 FAIL, 406 no data; tiers 1: 2,221, 2: 783, 3: 267, 4: 178, 5: 134, 6: 1,245. The pass share (62%) is higher than NC/VA's (48%) because Rust Belt values are low relative to rents — the same fact memo 63 §4 measured at county level. Three fields that the January framework supplied for NC/VA had to be approximated for the new counties, all descriptive rather than gating: the watchlist letter tier is P for a passing tract with ≥ 20 duplex-type structures (04b's cutoff), S for a passing tract with fewer, F otherwise (the NC/VA letters P/S/W/X0 also encode the January stock classification); `expected_sales_per_year` is the tract's structures × the county's Redfin sales ÷ summed tract stock (NC/VA median ratio 0.0028); `map_data` county categories come from the 696-county screen's class where the county is in it, else "A".

## 6. Pipeline changes

- `parsers.py`: the Redfin card regex `RFC` and the two detail-page address regexes were `(?:NC|VA)`; every Redfin list page outside NC/VA parsed to zero cards (checked on the live 19139 page: old pattern 0 matches, new pattern 41). They now accept the ten sweep states via a `STATES` constant. `key()` additionally collapses a repeated suffix token ("Main Street St" → "main st", "Arthur Ave Ave" → "arthur ave"), a Zillow habit that produced cross-portal duplicates in the smoke test.
- `sweep_remote.py`: `remote_setup(date, region, limit)` reads `sweep_zips_<region>.json`; `run_folder(date, region)` = `_sweeps/<date>` for ncva, `_sweeps/<date>-<region>` otherwise; every write goes there; `fetch_counts.json` carries the region.
- `weekly_sweep.py`: `--region` (default ncva, so the Monday task is unchanged in form); the run folder, ZIP list, tract-score files, watchlists and map data come from `constants.regions`; `ingest` marks every shortlisted row `detail_pending` in the seen index and re-shortlists pending rows on later runs until a detail page has been fetched (`prep` clears the flag), sorting the shortlist by rent-to-price so the capped fetch takes the best first; `underwrite` resolves tax and drive by ZIP before city and state, applies the rental adjustment, the tier bar, tier costs and the jurisdiction flag, writes `gate5_bar_pct`, `distance_tier`, `drive_h_google_eq`, `tier_costs_assumed`, `jurisdiction_flag` and `region` to `results.csv`, tags folded rows' events with `[<region>]`, re-scores only its own region's same-date rows, and writes a `region` column to the tally (`--dry` no longer touches the tally).

## 9. Operating the four routines

- Najum creates three scheduled tasks in Claude Code locally from `_pipeline/weekly_task_prompt_phila.md` (Tuesday 6:43 AM), `_pipeline/weekly_task_prompt_pitt.md` (Wednesday) and `_pipeline/weekly_task_prompt_ohio.md` (Thursday), and replaces the Monday task's instructions with `_pipeline/weekly_task_prompt_ncva.md` (identical in substance to the current prompt plus `--region=ncva` and the backlog note). The prompts differ only in region, weekday, run-folder suffix and the detail cap (40 for NC/VA, 50 for the regions).
- First-run expectations: a dense region's first run sees every listing as new; from the smoke test, the Tuesday region will shortlist on the order of 500–800 rows and detail 50; the backlog drains at 50 a week in rent-to-price order while new and price-cut rows join the front. "Constants needed" will list many CrimeGrade letters on the first runs (one lookup per ZIP, cached in `crime_zip` thereafter); tax rates and drive hours are pre-filled for every queued ZIP.
- Budget: the fetch legs are the same size as Monday (200 list pages ≈ 13–20 s per 40-page cell; 50 detail pages ≈ 50 s); the 60-minute cap is governed by the photo judgments.
- File 14 stays the single board; regional rows carry `[phila]`, `[pitt]` or `[ohio]` in their event text and their jurisdiction flag at the head of the note, and Najum reviews UNREVIEWED rows as before.
